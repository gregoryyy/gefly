## Question and method

Which individual POH values does the project need now, and what are they? User decision
(2026-11-01 13:59): extract only the needed values as cited facts; no Cessna text, whole
table or figure. Needs come from the draft Exercise 01 (`docs/design/EXERCISE-01.md`,
branch `design/exercise-criteria-1-5`) and its source note: (1) the airspeed calibration
that blocks every KIAS criterion, and (2) performance values for judging plausibility at
EDFE-like conditions.

No deep research skill ran. Fallback: the S17 copy already downloaded to a session
scratch directory outside the repository (SHA-256 prefix `420dbdaf5517a7a0`, identical to
the [172S sources](2026-11-01-c172s-fr172-sources.md) and Exercise 01 notes) was converted
with `pdftotext -layout` to locate pages, then each cited page was rendered with
`pdftoppm` and read visually; the text layer is an OCR scan with errors (for example
"14 7" for 147), so only the image reading is used. All extracted values are printed
numbers in tables (`reading: tabulated`); none was read off a graph.

## Findings (what was extracted)

The JSON file holds the values; this summary lists only what was taken and why.

1. **Airspeed calibration, normal static source** (Figure 5-1 sheet 1, p. 5-9; condition:
   power for level flight or maximum-power descent). Extracted IAS→CAS pairs: flaps up
   50–110 KIAS in 10 kt steps (7 points), flaps 10° 50–90 KIAS (5), flaps 30° 50–80 KIAS
   (4); 16 of the 26 pairs on the sheet. Ranges follow the Exercise 01 IAS bands
   (rotation 50–70, climb 70–90, flaps-up approach 65–85, flaps-30 final 60–80, downwind
   target TBD). Observed: CAS exceeds IAS by about 6–7 kt at 50 KIAS and IAS exceeds CAS
   by 3 kt at 90–110 KIAS flaps up.
2. **Stall speeds** (Figure 5-3, p. 5-12): 2,550 lb, power off, wings level, flaps up/10°/30°,
   KIAS and KCAS, both most rearward and most forward CG (12 values). POH notes: KIAS
   approximate; up to 230 ft altitude loss in recovery.
3. **Take-off** (Figure 5-5 sheet 1, p. 5-14): short-field technique, 2,550 lb, flaps 10°,
   full throttle before brake release, paved level dry runway, zero wind, lift-off
   51 KIAS, 56 KIAS at 50 ft. Ground roll and total over 50 ft at sea level and 1,000 ft
   pressure altitude, each at 10 °C and 20 °C (8 values).
4. **Landing** (Figure 5-11, p. 5-23/5-24): short-field technique, 2,550 lb, flaps 30°,
   power off, maximum braking, paved level dry runway, zero wind, 61 KIAS at 50 ft; same
   four condition points (8 values).
5. **Maximum rate of climb** (Figure 5-6, p. 5-17): 2,550 lb, flaps up, full throttle;
   climb speed and rate at sea level and 2,000 ft, each at 0 °C and 20 °C (6 values).
6. **Correction notes**, summarised in our words: headwind −10 % per 9 kt; tailwind up to
   10 kt +10 % per 2 kt; dry grass adds 15 % (take-off) or 45 % (landing) of the ground-roll
   figure; flaps-up landing +9 KIAS approach and about 35 % longer distances; reading the
   next higher weight/altitude/temperature gives a conservative figure (p. 5-4); data
   come from flight tests with airplane and engine in good condition and average piloting
   technique (p. 5-3).

Condition choice: EDFE elevation is unresolved at 371–387 ft
([EDFE sources](2026-11-01-edfe-aerodrome-sources.md)); ISA is 15 °C at sea level and
about 13 °C at 1,000 ft and 11 °C at 2,000 ft. The tables have no ISA column, so the
points bracketing those conditions were taken instead of an interpolated value; any
interpolation would be a `derived` value with its method recorded.

Cross-check (inference): linear interpolation of the extracted points at sea level and
15 °C reproduces the S17 introduction-page summary values recorded in the
[172S sources](2026-11-01-c172s-fr172-sources.md) (take-off 960/1,630 ft, landing
575/1,335 ft, climb 730 fpm), and the stall KCAS values match its 53/48 KCAS.

## Not found or not extracted

- **Normal-technique take-off and landing distances** are not in S17 Section 5; only
  short-field charts exist. Exercise 01 uses normal procedures (flaps 0–10° take-off), so
  the short-field values are a reference with different technique, not a target.
- No flaps-20° calibration (relevant to the balked-landing configuration).
- Alternate static source (p. 5-10), other bank angles, other weights (2,400/2,200 lb
  take-off sheets), altitudes and temperatures: not needed now; listed in `not_extracted`.

## POH-internal discrepancy

p. 2-4 pairs VFE 10° flaps 110 KIAS with 107 KCAS, while the Figure 5-1 flaps-10° row
gives 109 KCAS at 110 KIAS (point read but not extracted into the data). Calibration
conditions differ (p. 5-9 states a power condition; p. 2-4 none), so this is recorded as
an open point, not resolved. Exercise 01 gates VFE in KCAS from p. 2-4.

## Rights

- Cessna copyright (1998) covers the manual's text, layout, figures and tables; the
  befa.org hosting grants no reuse right. Individual performance numbers are facts; we
  record them with page citations, write all notes in our own words, and reproduce no
  table, figure, layout or text. No copy of the manual is in the repository.
- **Selectivity**: about 70 numbers from five figures, each chosen for a stated need and
  each figure only partially taken (calibration 16 of 26 pairs; take-off and landing 4 of
  45 condition points per chart; climb 4 of 27 rates; stall 0°-bank columns only).
- **EU database right** (Directive 96/9/EC, Art. 7): prohibits extraction or re-use of a
  *substantial* part of a database, and repeated systematic extraction of insubstantial
  parts that conflicts with normal exploitation (Art. 7(5)). Whether a POH chart is a
  protected database is uncertain; the right requires substantial investment in the
  contents and, under Art. 11, a maker who is an EU national or established in the EU,
  which a US manufacturer would not obviously meet. We nevertheless keep extraction
  insubstantial and need-driven, and do not plan to complete the tables piecemeal.
  This is a research assessment, not legal advice; Q5 remains the rights decision point.

## Implications (proposal)

- Exercise 01 can replace "IAS blocked" with the cited calibration points for its bands,
  stating the interpolation method and that only the normal static source is covered.
- Plausibility checks for the simulator may compare against these points with the POH's
  own conditions; deviations must not be judged against a tolerance the POH does not give.
- A model built on the short-field charts must state the technique difference.
