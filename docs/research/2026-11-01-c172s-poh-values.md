## Question and method

Which individual POH values does the project need now, and what are they? User decision
(2026-11-01 13:59): extract only the needed values as cited facts; no Cessna text, whole
table or figure. Needs come from the draft Exercise 01 (`docs/design/EXERCISE-01.md`,
branch `design/exercise-criteria-1-5`) and its source note: (1) the airspeed calibration
that blocks every KIAS criterion, and (2) performance values for judging plausibility at
EDFE-like conditions.

No deep research skill ran. Fallback: the S17 copy already downloaded to a session
scratch directory outside the repository (SHA-256 prefix `420dbdaf5517a7a0`, identical to
the [172S sources](2026-11-01-c172s-fr172-sources.md) and Exercise 01 notes) was converted
with `pdftotext -layout` to locate pages, then each cited page was rendered with
`pdftoppm` and read visually; the text layer is an OCR scan with errors (for example
"14 7" for 147), so only the image reading is used. All extracted values are printed
numbers in tables (`reading: tabulated`); none was read off a graph.

## Findings (what was extracted)

The JSON file holds the values; this summary lists only what was taken and why.

1. **Airspeed calibration, normal static source** (Figure 5-1 sheet 1, p. 5-9; condition:
   power for level flight or maximum-power descent). Extracted IAS→CAS pairs: flaps up
   50–110 KIAS in 10 kt steps (7 points), flaps 10° 50–90 KIAS (5), flaps 30° 50–80 KIAS
   (4); 16 of the 26 pairs on the sheet. Ranges follow the Exercise 01 IAS bands
   (rotation 50–70, climb 70–90, flaps-up approach 65–85, flaps-30 final 60–80, downwind
   target TBD). Observed: CAS exceeds IAS by about 6–7 kt at 50 KIAS and IAS exceeds CAS
   by 3 kt at 90–110 KIAS flaps up.
2. **Stall speeds** (Figure 5-3, p. 5-12): 2,550 lb, power off, wings level, flaps up/10°/30°,
   KIAS and KCAS, both most rearward and most forward CG (12 values). POH notes: KIAS
   approximate; up to 230 ft altitude loss in recovery.
3. **Take-off** (Figure 5-5 sheet 1, p. 5-14): short-field technique, 2,550 lb, flaps 10°,
   full throttle before brake release, paved level dry runway, zero wind, lift-off
   51 KIAS, 56 KIAS at 50 ft. Ground roll and total over 50 ft at sea level and 1,000 ft
   pressure altitude, each at 10 °C and 20 °C (8 values).
4. **Landing** (Figure 5-11, p. 5-23/5-24): short-field technique, 2,550 lb, flaps 30°,
   power off, maximum braking, paved level dry runway, zero wind, 61 KIAS at 50 ft; same
   four condition points (8 values).
5. **Maximum rate of climb** (Figure 5-6, p. 5-17): 2,550 lb, flaps up, full throttle;
   climb speed and rate at sea level and 2,000 ft, each at 0 °C and 20 °C (6 values).
6. **Correction notes**, summarised in our words: headwind −10 % per 9 kt; tailwind up to
   10 kt +10 % per 2 kt; dry grass adds 15 % (take-off) or 45 % (landing) of the ground-roll
   figure; flaps-up landing +9 KIAS approach and about 35 % longer distances; reading the
   next higher weight/altitude/temperature gives a conservative figure (p. 5-4); data
   come from flight tests with airplane and engine in good condition and average piloting
   technique (p. 5-3).

Condition choice: EDFE elevation is unresolved at 371–387 ft
([EDFE sources](2026-11-01-edfe-aerodrome-sources.md)); ISA is 15 °C at sea level and
about 13 °C at 1,000 ft and 11 °C at 2,000 ft. The tables have no ISA column, so the
points bracketing those conditions were taken instead of an interpolated value; any
interpolation would be a `derived` value with its method recorded.

Cross-check (inference): linear interpolation of the extracted points at sea level and
15 °C reproduces the S17 introduction-page summary values recorded in the
[172S sources](2026-11-01-c172s-fr172-sources.md) (take-off 960/1,630 ft, landing
575/1,335 ft, climb 730 fpm), and the stall KCAS values match its 53/48 KCAS.

## POH-internal discrepancy

p. 2-4 pairs VFE 10° flaps 110 KIAS with 107 KCAS, while the Figure 5-1 flaps-10° row
gives 109 KCAS at 110 KIAS (point read but not extracted into the data). Calibration
conditions differ (p. 5-9 states a power condition; p. 2-4 none), so this is recorded as
an open point, not resolved. Exercise 01 gates VFE in KCAS from p. 2-4.

## Rights

- Cessna copyright (1998) covers the manual's text, layout, figures and tables; the
  befa.org hosting grants no reuse right. Individual performance numbers are facts; we
  record them with page citations, write all notes in our own words, and reproduce no
  table, figure, layout or text. No copy of the manual is in the repository.
- **Selectivity**: 66 numbers from five figures, each chosen for a stated need. Share of
  each figure's printed data values (headings and conditions not counted):

  | Figure (page) | Extracted / printed values | Share |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 5-1 sheet 1, calibration (5-9) | 32 / 52 (16 of 26 IAS/CAS pairs) | 62 % |
  | 5-3, stall speeds (5-12) | 12 / 48 (0° bank, both CG tables) | 25 % |
  | 5-5 sheet 1, take-off (5-14) | 8 / 90 | 9 % |
  | 5-11, landing (5-23/5-24) | 8 / 90 | 9 % |
  | 5-6, climb (5-17) | 6 / 34 (2 of 7 speeds, 4 of 27 rates) | 18 % |

  The calibration share is high because the exercise's speed bands cover most of the
  sheet. It is the figure most exposed to a "substantial part" argument and the first
  candidate for trimming if the Q5 rights judgement asks for it.
- **No piecemeal completion (limit)**: later extractions must each name a concrete need
  and must not, alone or added to earlier ones, assemble a whole table or figure (or
  nearly all of one), across any number of commits or files. Before an extraction, add up
  what is already in the repository per figure; if the total would exceed what a stated
  need requires, or approach a complete table, stop and ask the orchestrator (GAPS Q5).
- **EU database right** (Directive 96/9/EC, Art. 7): prohibits extraction or re-use of a
  *substantial* part of a database, and repeated systematic extraction of insubstantial
  parts that conflicts with normal exploitation (Art. 7(5)). Whether a POH chart is a
  protected database is uncertain; the right requires substantial investment in the
  contents and, under Art. 11, a maker who is an EU national or established in the EU,
  which a US manufacturer would not obviously meet. We nevertheless keep extraction
  insubstantial and need-driven, and do not plan to complete the tables piecemeal.
  This is a research assessment, not legal advice; Q5 remains the rights decision point.

## Engine, cruise and glide extraction

Added 2026-11-01 (Europe/Berlin) by the same role, for calibrating a JSBSim piston engine
and airframe in the circuit regime (calibration set of the
[172 aero-data note](https://github.com/gregoryyy/gefly/blob/research/c172-aero-data/docs/research/2026-11-01-c172-aero-data-sources.md),
branch `research/c172-aero-data`, source A14 and open point 5). User decision
2026-11-01 18:43 (GAPS Q5): extract only the needed points of the Lycoming IO-360-L2A
power curve as cited facts, never the whole chart; the no-piecemeal limit above applies.

**Sources and method.** A14: Lycoming Operator's Manual P/N 60297-12, 8th edition
October 2005; its revision record lists revision 60297-12-5 (December 2009) as current,
which this copy includes (the aero-data note gives "pages revised to Mar 2009"; the
cited pages 2-2 and 3-40 carry no revision mark). Copy: EAA chapter 245 PDF, SHA-256
prefix `150de015b2d1bce7` (same file as in the aero-data note, re-used from the session
scratch directory, not downloaded again). S17 as above. Pages were rendered with
`pdftoppm` (chart pages at 400 dpi); chart readings use pixel positions of the detected
grid lines and of the traced curve, with the curve identified by its printed label and
cross-checked by eye. Values in tables were read on the page image.

**Findings.**

1. *Ratings check* (A14 p. 2-2): IO-360-L2A, FAA TC 1E10, 160 hp at 2400 rpm, with the
   footnote "alternate rating of 180 HP at 2700 RPM"; displacement 361.0 in³. Agrees with
   S9 (134 kW / 180 HP at 2700 rpm, alternate rating) and the manifest TCDS values.
2. *Curve 13516-A* (A14 Figure 3-25, p. 3-40; maximum-power mixture, fuel minimum 91/96,
   drawn for the 160 hp / 2400 rpm rating; sea-level panel: brake hp against absolute
   manifold pressure 18–29.5 inHg for 1600–2700 rpm, with 2500–2700 rpm dashed; the
   legend does not explain the dashing). Read from chart (±2 hp, ±0.1 inHg):
   full throttle (upper end of each sea-level curve) 2000 rpm 140 hp at 29.5 inHg,
   2400 rpm 160 hp at 29.1 inHg (= normal rating, check passed), 2500 rpm 166 hp at
   29.1 inHg (±3 hp: the curve ends in a short dash), 2700 rpm 180 hp at 28.7 inHg (curve
   ends at a small circle on the 180 hp line, = alternate rating). Part throttle:
   2000 rpm 77 hp at 20 inHg and 104 hp at 24 inHg; 2400 rpm 136 hp at 26 inHg.
   **2300 rpm: no curve on the chart**, recorded as `unknown` with a TODO; interpolating
   between 2200 and 2400 rpm would be a derived modelling choice.
3. *Cross-check, not recorded as data*: the altitude panel's full-throttle lines at sea
   level give 2000 rpm 140.5, 2200 rpm 152.7 (sea-level panel curve end 151.3) and
   2400 rpm 160.3 hp; agreement within 1.5 hp. The 2500–2700 rpm altitude lines start
   at about 1,000 ft, so they do not cross-check the sea-level ends.
4. *POH cruise* (S17 Figure 5-8 sheet 1, p. 5-19; 2550 lb, recommended lean mixture):
   pressure altitude 2000 ft (the lowest block, closest to the EDFE circuit), standard
   temperature, all six rows: 2550 rpm 77 % 118 KTAS 10.5 gph; 2500: 73 %, 115, 9.9;
   2400: 64 %, 110, 9.0; 2300: 57 %, 104, 8.1; 2200: 50 %, 97, 7.3; 2100: 44 %, 90, 6.6.
   The page does not say which power the percentage refers to (inference: the 180 hp
   rating; not recorded as a value). The POH rates the engine 180 BHP at 2700 rpm
   (pp. 1-4, 2-5) but does not tie the table's percentages to it; the data file's
   `pending` says the basis is unknown and must not be taken as the Lycoming 160 hp. The text layer shows OCR errors ("11 .1"); the
   image reading is used.
5. *Fuel curve 13518* (A14 Figure 3-9, p. 3-24; added after the user decision
   2026-11-01 19:04, GAPS Q5, allowing selected fuel-chart points). Part-throttle fuel
   consumption in lb/h against actual brake hp (80–160 hp), mixture manually set to best
   power (2400, 2200 rpm) or best economy (2400, 2200, 2000, 1800 rpm), percent-rated-power
   marks 55/65/75 % of the 160 hp rating; the curve number may be cut at the page edge.
   Read from chart (±1 lb/h; the scan's horizontal grid spacing varies by up to 10 %, so
   readings interpolate between the nearest grid lines): 2400 rpm best power 60.5 lb/h at
   120 hp and 75.6 lb/h at the curve end, 160 hp; 2400 rpm best economy 51.9 lb/h at its
   curve end, 120 hp. Inference, not recorded as data: BSFC about 0.50 (best power, 120 hp),
   0.47 (best power, 160 hp) and 0.43 lb/(hp·h) (best economy, 120 hp). **2700 rpm: no
   curve** (`unknown` with TODO); full-power fuel flow for the 180 hp rating needs another
   source or a declared extrapolation.
6. *POH glide*: maximum glide 68 KIAS (p. 3-3, page mark May 30/00; also in the Figure
   3-1 conditions box). Figure 3-1 (p. 3-14, Revision 4): conditions 68 KIAS, propeller
   windmilling, flaps up, zero wind; no weight stated. The chart is one straight line
   through the origin; read at the 12,000 ft top border: 17.97 nm, recorded 18.0 ±0.2 nm.
   Derived glide ratio 18.0 nm / 12,000 ft = 9.11 (±0.1), so about 1.5 nm per 1,000 ft.

**Applicability limits.** Lycoming data are for an uninstalled engine at standard sea-level
conditions with maximum-power mixture; installation losses, the fixed-pitch McCauley
propeller (TCDS static full-throttle rpm 2300–2400) and leaning are not included, so the
curve points calibrate the engine model only together with a propeller/installation
model. The cruise table uses recommended lean mixture and includes the installation, so
it is the airframe-plus-engine calibration target. The chart is drawn for the 160 hp
rating; using its 2500–2700 rpm curves for the 172S's 180 hp alternate rating is
supported by the 2700 rpm curve ending at 180 hp, but no Lycoming statement of that use
was read.

## Implications (proposal)

  own conditions; deviations must not be judged against a tolerance the POH does not give.
- A model built on the short-field charts must state the technique difference.
