aircraft variations inside the existing aeroplane PPL/CPL curriculum?

## Factual distinctions

- **Sailplanes:** S1 identifies the Sailplane Pilot Licence, SPL, under Part-SFCL.
  Sailplane/TMG privileges are addressed separately in SFCL.150. Launch-method
  training is distinct in SFCL.155; examples include winch, aerotow and self-launch.
  Do not assume self-launch and TMG privileges are interchangeable.
- **German ultralights:** S2/S3 identify a national *Luftfahrerschein für
  Luftsportgeräteführer*, with scope tied to the entered aircraft/device category.
  §42 distinguishes, for example, aerodynamic-control and weight-shift ultralights.
  This is not evidence of a universal EASA ultralight licence.
- **Existing licence holders:** S4 describes category-specific training/assessment
  routes, including for EASA PPL/LAPL holders. Shared skills do not establish
  automatic licence conversion, universal credit or simulator-training credit.
- **Acronyms:** EASA's sailplane SPL and S5's German “Sportpilotenlizenz” usage
  cannot safely share an unqualified `SPL` course identifier. Store full licence
  name, authority/jurisdiction, aircraft category and applicable privileges.

## Proposed roadmap and acceptance implications

Add **distinct licence-specific sailplane and ultralight tracks** to the roadmap,
alongside the aeroplane PPL/CPL path. Keep each track's jurisdiction, source edition,
objectives, aircraft/model, prerequisites, instructor review and acceptance evidence
explicit. The new request does not choose launch methods, an ultralight category,
course ordering or permission to reuse a publisher's examination-question bank.

Sailplane examples to scope later: energy management, soaring/weather interpretation,
approach/landing, and selected launch-specific exercises; TMG operation would have
its own declared equipment and privilege scope. Ultralight examples: handling,
cross-country planning, checks and abnormal situations appropriate to one selected
national category and actual aircraft. These are proposals, not accepted syllabi.

Reuse common maps, weather, communication, replay and teaching infrastructure where
appropriate, but validate the track's aircraft behavior and exercise criteria
independently. A C172 model or completed PPL exercise is insufficient evidence for
a glider launch, a weight-shift ultralight or a different licence's learning goal.

Before implementation, resolve target country/framework, first aircraft/category,
launch method/TMG scope where applicable, source rights and qualified review.
Maintain traceability from each lesson to the selected syllabus and reference
edition; distinguish supplementary practice from approved or creditable training.

Review outcome: enough evidence to add separate roadmap tracks and an explicit
licence/curriculum requirement; insufficient to accept a syllabus, legal eligibility
or fidelity/learning-transfer claims. No copyrighted manuals or question banks were
